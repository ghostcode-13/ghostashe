What lives Between

Before electricity convinced us that darkness was something to be defeated, our ancestors knew better. They left offerings at crossroads. They whispered prayers before sleep. Not out of superstition but out of respect for what shared the world with them.

Science calls it hyperactive agency detection, our brain's oldest survival mechanism. We evolved to sense presence, to feel watched, to hear intention in every sound. It kept us alive for thousands of years.
But somewhere along the way, we decided it was madness. The coldness that follows you down a familiar hallway. The shadow that moves a second too late. The feeling of being observed in an empty room. Psychology says your brain is misfiring.

But what if it isn't? What if the oldest part of you, the part that survived ice ages and predators and endless nights without light, is the only part still paying attention? Ghost Ashe exists in that frequency. These images are not horror. They are memories. A reminder that the world you see is only half of what is there. The other half has always been watching.
